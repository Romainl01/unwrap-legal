# Privacy Policy

**Unwrap**
Version 1.0, 20 November 2026

> Every factual claim in this document was written against the app's source
> code rather than from a template.

---

## The short version

Unwrap is an app for couples. Every evening it deals three sealed cards, you
pick one, scratch it, and find a small surprise to do for your partner: a
gesture, a date idea, something flirty, or, if you asked for it, something hot.

**There is no account.** You never sign up, and there is no server of ours that
holds a profile of you.

**What you tell the app stays on your phone.** Your names, your answers, your
love languages and the cards you scratched are stored on the device and are
never sent to us. The most intimate answers, the ones that come after the
screen that says "What you say next stays here", never leave the phone at all,
in any form.

Two things do leave, and both are deliberate:

- **usage reports**, so we can see which screens work and where people give up.
  They contain the choices you make on some setup screens (for example, which
  love language you put first), never a name, never the text of a card, and
  never the intimate answers. Section 3.1 lists them;
- **your purchase**, if you subscribe, so the app knows you have access.
  Section 3.2.

No advertising, no tracking across apps, no data sold.

**Your names never leave the phone.** Not to us, not in a report. When you type
a name, the report says only how many letters it has.

**The intimate answers never leave the phone.** The answers you give after the
screen titled "What you say next stays here" (how often, who makes the first
move, positions, what you would like to try, what turns you on) are read by the
app to choose and personalise your cards, on the phone. No report contains
them, and no code path sends them anywhere. Usage reports do record that the
screens of that block were *shown*, the same way every screen is counted, but
never what you answered on them.

**Your partner never sees your answers.** Unwrap runs on one phone. Nothing is
shared with a second device or a second person.

**Backups.** Like any app's data, what Unwrap stores is included in your
iPhone's own backups (iCloud or your computer) if you have those turned on.
Those backups are yours and Apple's, under Apple's policy; we cannot read them.

## 3. What leaves your phone

### 3.1 How the app is used

The app uses **PostHog** to count how it is used. Each report carries a random
identifier that PostHog creates on your phone the first time the app opens. It
names no one, and erasing everything in Settings replaces it with a new one.

The reports contain:

- **which screens you open** and which steps of the setup you reach, with how
  long each step took;
- **when the app is installed, opened, updated, or sent to the background**;
- **the evening's cards**: which card you picked from the three, its place in
  the fan, how many you looked at first, whether you scratched it, how long the
  scratch took, whether you asked for a new draw, and whether you marked it as
  done. A card is identified by an internal code and its category (for
  example, "sweet" or "hot"), **never by its text**;
- **some of your setup answers**, as choices from a list, never as anything you
  typed:
  - your age range (for example, "25 to 34") and whether you confirmed you are
    18 or older;
  - your gender and your partner's, as you chose them;
  - how long you have been together, as a range (less than a year, 1 to 3
    years, 3 to 10, more than 10), never the date;
  - how you usually spend your weeknights, when you last surprised your
    partner, what gets in the way, and what has faded between you (for example,
    "laughs" or "flirting");
  - how happy you are in the relationship, on a scale of 1 to 5;
  - what you want from the app (for example, "surprise", "romance", "spice");
  - the kinds of surprise in your mix (sweet, dates, flirty, hot), and whether
    your first card was the hot one or the classic one;
  - the order of your love languages and your partner's;
  - how many letters your names have, never the names;
- **what you change in Settings**: the kinds of surprise you switch on or off,
  reordering a love language, changing your reminder time or turning it off,
  correcting a name (only that it changed, never the name), and erasing
  everything;
- **your answers about notifications and rating**: whether you allowed the
  evening reminder and at what time, and how you answered "Enjoying it so
  far?" (Love it, or Not yet), and whether Apple's rating sheet could be shown;
- **the subscription screen**: when it is shown, how far you scrolled, which
  plan you selected, which questions you opened, and whether a purchase or a
  restore started, succeeded or failed;
- **errors the app did not expect**: the error message and where in the app's
  code it happened, so we can fix it;
- **technical context**: the app version, your iPhone model, your iOS version,
  your language and time zone, your screen size, and an approximate location
  (country and city) that PostHog works out from the network address of the
  request.

Reports **never** contain: your name or your partner's, the day you got
together, the text of any card, the intimate answers of section 2, an email
address, a phone number, a contact, a photo, or a location from your phone's
GPS (the app never asks for location).

We use this to improve the app, on the basis of our legitimate interest. You can
object at any time by writing to romainlagrange33@gmail.com, and we will delete
the reports already recorded. Because there is no account and the identifier
names no one, we cannot look you up by name or email: tell us roughly when you
installed the app and which iPhone model you use, and we will find your reports
with you. Erasing everything in the app (section 6) also cuts the link between
what you do next and what was reported before.

### 3.2 Your subscription

Unwrap Pro is sold by Apple, through the App Store. We never see your card,
your billing address, or your Apple Account.

The app uses **RevenueCat** to check whether your subscription is active.

- **What RevenueCat receives:** your App Store purchase and renewal history for
  this app, an anonymous identifier RevenueCat creates itself, the usage report
  identifier of section 3.1 (so a purchase can be matched to the same
  anonymous person in our usage reports), and the ordinary technical context of
  the request: your device model, your iOS version, the app version and the
  country of your App Store account.
- **What it never receives:** your names, your answers, your cards.

Your purchase history also lives with Apple, under Apple's own policy, because
Apple is the merchant. **Cancelling** is done in your iPhone's Settings, not by
us.

### 3.3 The evening reminder

If you allow it, the evening reminder is a **local notification**: it is
scheduled on your phone, by your phone. It never goes through a server, and the
app has no push token. Its text never says what your card is, because a
notification can appear on a locked screen in front of anyone. You can change or
turn it off in Settings, Daily reminder.

### 3.4 Asking Apple whether the app is published

To decide whether to show the rating question, the app asks the public App
Store directory whether this version of Unwrap is already published, and reads
its public rating. The request contains the app's identifier and a country
code, nothing about you.

### 3.5 What we do not collect

No account. No advertising, and no advertising identifier: the app never asks
for permission to track you, because it does not track you, in the App Store's
sense or any other. Nothing is shared with advertisers or data brokers, and
nothing is combined with data from other companies' apps. No separate crash
reporting service: errors travel with the usage reports, to PostHog. No
location, no contacts, no photos, no camera, no microphone, no Apple Health.

We do not sell data.

## 5. How long we keep it

What stays on your phone stays until you erase it or delete the app.

Usage reports are kept for as long as they help us improve the app, and in any
case no longer than PostHog keeps them for our project. To have yours deleted,
write to us (section 3.1).

Purchase records are kept by RevenueCat and Apple for as long as they are
needed to manage your subscription and for the periods their own policies and
the law require.

---

## 6. Erasing your data

**In the app: Settings, then Erase everything.** It is immediate and it cannot
be undone.

When you do that:

- your names, your answers, your love languages and every card you scratched
  are deleted from the phone;
- the evening reminder is cancelled;
- the usage report identifier is replaced by a new one, so nothing after that
  moment is linked to what came before.

Deleting the app also deletes everything it stored on the phone.

Neither of those cancels a subscription: that is done in your iPhone's
Settings, under your name, Subscriptions. Reports already sent to PostHog stay
there until you ask us to delete them.

## 7. Your rights

If you are in the EU or the UK, the GDPR gives you the right to access, correct,
export, delete, or object to the processing of your personal data, and the right
to complain to a supervisory authority (in France, the CNIL).

In practice, for this app:

- **Everything personal is on your phone**, where you can see it, change it in
  Settings, and erase it with one button.
- **Usage reports**: write to us to access them, object to them or have them
  deleted. As there is no account, we will need to know which identifier is
  yours, and we will help you find it.
- **Purchases**: your purchase history is held by Apple, and can be managed
  from your Apple Account.

## 8. Age

Unwrap is meant for adults in a relationship. It is not directed at children
under 13, and we do not knowingly collect anything from them.

Hot cards, and the setup questions that go with them, are only for people who
confirm they are 18 or older. If you say you are under 18, Hot is switched off
and those questions are skipped.
